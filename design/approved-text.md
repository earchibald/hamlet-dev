# The text, after the panel

Three Sonnet reviewers read `text-pack.md`: one for humanity, one for plain English, one for
readability in a scrolling log. Their reviews are in `review-humanity.md`, `review-plain.md` and
`review-readability.md`. A second pack of twenty more old-tongue meanings went through the same
three reviewers; those reviews are `review2-humanity.md`, `review2-plain.md` and
`review2-readability.md`.

**The user approved every set in this file. Use these words exactly. Do not improve them.**

Where the panel split, the majority held. Three lines were split two to one and kept: "the Day We
Lost One", "the Night the Fire Ran", "the Cold That Stayed".

## Set A2: twenty more old-tongue meanings

The kinds each one suits are in `land-word-kinds.md`, which is the table task 1 must use.

| Kind | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Water | where the fish gather · the place we drink · the green shallows · where the reeds thicken |
| Crossings | the stepping stones · the low bank · the sure footing |
| Hills | the sunless side · the side the wind takes · the three stones · the long climb |
| Caves | where the roof drips · the still air · the crack in the rock · where the echo lives |
| Groves | the trees no one has cut · where the nuts fall · the fallen trunks · where the sap runs |
| Any kind | where winter shows first |

Two rules came out of this second pass and bind any phrase added later. A phrase must not repeat
the kind word in the frame sentence, so no crossing is called "the safe crossing". And a phrase
must not repeat an image already in the pool, which is what removed "the cold breath", "the narrow
way in", "the tall shade" and "the burnt stumps".

## Set A: the first ten

Changed by the panel: "the dry room" to "the dry hole" (a namer of old would not say room); "the
long hollow" to "the low crawl" ("hollow" is the name of another kind); "the high back" to "the
high ridge"; "the leaning wood" to "where the branches tangle" (it repeated the image in "the
crooked trees"); "the slow water" to "the slow current" (it stuttered against the frame sentence,
"called this water").

## Set B: seven lines for a camp taking its name

One is drawn at random. `X` is the new name. The reason clause already exists and is appended to
each: "for Esk, who made the camp", "for the reed by the water".

1. The camp has a name now: X, why.
2. They start to call the camp X, why.
3. The camp is X now, why.
4. The camp goes by X now, why.
5. Nobody calls it the camp any more. It is X, why.
6. They have agreed on a name for the camp: X, why.
7. They say the new name all evening: X, why.

Cut: "They have a name for the camp" (it repeated line 6). Changed: "where they live" to "the
camp"; "They call the camp X now" to "The camp goes by X now", because the original was built on
the same skeleton as the line that names a patch of ground; "The camp celebrates its new name" to
"They say the new name all evening", because a camp cannot celebrate.

None of these uses the word village. A separate line already announces that.

## Set C: twenty-one names for a night

| Tag | Phrases |
|---|---|
| `wolf` | the Night of the Wolf · the Night the Wolves Came · the Night of Teeth |
| `fire` | the Night the Fire Ran · the Night It Burned · the Night the Pine Burned |
| `frost` | the Night of the Long Frost · the Night the Water Froze · the Cold That Stayed |
| `sprite` | the Night of Lights · the Night the Valley Glowed · the Night Nobody Slept |
| `found` | the Day They Left · the Day the Camp Split · the Morning the Camp Was Smaller |
| `death` | the Day We Lost One · the Day the Hearth Was Quiet · the Night the Food Went Cold |
| `birth` | the Day a Child Came · the Day the Camp Grew · the Morning of the Cradle |

One phrase already in the game changes: `frost` was "the Long Frost" and is now "the Night of the
Long Frost", because with no time word it reads as the name of a place. Every other first phrase
is the one the game already uses.

Changed by the panel: "the Night of Embers" to "the Night It Burned"; "the Night the Lights Came"
to "the Night the Valley Glowed"; "the Morning They Went" to "the Morning the Camp Was Smaller";
"the Day the Fire Was Quiet" to "the Day the Hearth Was Quiet", so a death is not read as a fire;
"the Night Nobody Ate" to "the Night the Food Went Cold". Each of those five repeated the shape of
another phrase in the set.
