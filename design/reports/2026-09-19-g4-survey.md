# G4 survey: where the time goes, and what the retune must move

Read-only survey for the G4 plan. Measured on this machine (darwin 27.2, Node `v26.9.0`) in
`/Users/earchibald/Worktrees/hamlet-tiers` at branch `tiers-g3`, commit `3620a2b`. Nothing in the tree
was changed. Scratch scripts and an instrumented copy of `src/sim/` are in
`/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-earchibald-Code-hamlet/376317b0-a8bc-4656-840b-31d5cb9f8aaa/scratchpad/g4survey/`.

## Summary: the five numbers that constrain the plan

| Fact | Number | Where |
|---|---|---|
| A world day costs, today | 122 to 603 ms (1000 ticks), rising with the population | part 1 |
| The same world day with only the constants changed | 86,400 ticks x today's per-tick cost = **26 to 52 s**, 5 to 10 times the spec's 5 s budget | part 2 |
| The irreducible per-tick floor (beings, camps and the cellular steps all switched off) | 1.7 to 4.0 us a tick = **0.15 to 0.35 s a world day** | part 2 |
| `updateBeing`'s per-tick head, if it stays per tick | 53 to 86 beings x 86,400 ticks x 0.79 us = **3.6 to 5.9 s a world day**, on its own over budget | part 2 |
| Everything else (choices, path searches, camp rules, cellular systems) is already priced per world day, not per tick | 150 to 420 ms a world day, unchanged by the retune | part 1, part 2 |

Every count printed below "per 1000 ticks" is also "per world day", because today a world day is 1000 ticks.
That is the single most useful frame for the retune: a count per world day mostly does not move, a cost per
tick multiplies by 86.4.

## Method and noise

- Seeds `r` and `beta`, run with `tests/lib/run.js`'s script god, profiled over a 1000-tick window at day 20 and at day 50.
- Self-time profiler with an explicit stack, so a child's time is subtracted from its parent. `bfs` self time is
  therefore not inside `taskStop`'s, and `chooseTask`'s is not inside `being.human`'s.
- The wrappers inflate the window. Instrumented wall time against an uninstrumented baseline of the same window:

| Seed, day | Uninstrumented (3 runs) | Instrumented | Inflation |
|---|---|---|---|
| r, 20 | 148.6, 147.6, 153.8 ms | 169.8 ms | +13% |
| r, 50 | 377.6, 375.5, 386.5 ms | 427.9 ms | +13% |
| beta, 20 | 142.9, 153.2, 138.9 ms | 155.9 ms | +5% |
| beta, 50 | 335.7, 337.2, 331.0 ms | 380.1 ms | +13% |

- The uninstrumented numbers repeated within 4% over three runs, so the machine was quiet enough. No other
  session's test run was seen to move a number; if one had, the spread above would be wider.
- `tests/soak.js` was not run. The 70-day cost curve in part 2 is a plain `step()` loop, not the soak.

**92% of the whole stream is `growPlants`** (`world.js:709-736`), which samples `CLOCK.plant.samples` = 60
random tiles every tick. Moving the cellular systems to a once-a-world-minute beat rewrites the stream
wholesale. That is allowed in G4 (the golden is blessed once), but it means no intermediate commit of G4
can keep the golden, so the plan should move the beat and the units in one step, not two.

The per-tick head of `updateBeing` draws **almost nothing**: the only draw in `beings.js:334-379` is the
old-age roll at line 359, and it sits behind `ageDays(a) > LIFE[a.species].life`, which short-circuits for
every being inside its span. Lazy evaluation of the head is therefore nearly free of stream consequences;
what it changes is the order of the draws that remain, not their count.

### Under the spec's rules

Three probes, measured by switching parts off in a copy of `src/sim/` (the copy is a cost probe, not a valid
world: switching the cellular systems off starves people and makes them re-plan, which is why "cell off" can
read *higher* than "nothing off"; only the last row is a clean number).

| Probe, seed r day 50 | ms per 1000 ticks |
|---|---|
| nothing off | 359.8 |
| cellular off | 673.6 (confounded: a world with no plants behaves differently) |
| cellular and camps off | 541.3 (confounded the same way) |
| cellular, camps and every being off | **4.00** |

Seed r day 20: 1.72. Seed beta day 20: 2.39. Seed beta day 50: 2.29.

So **the per-tick floor of `step()` with nobody in it is 1.7 to 4.0 us**: the `tick++`, the eleven `updateWorld`
calls returning at once, the `for (const a of beings)` scan over all 61 to 93 records, and the prune check.
At 86,400 ticks that is **0.15 to 0.35 s a world day**. It is not the problem.

Now the parts, priced per world day.

| Work | Today, per world day | Under G4, per world day | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cellular systems (`spreadFire`, `updateWeather`, `growPlants`, `strayLightning`, `rotCarcasses`, `groveTick`) | 11.6 to 16.7 ms | **the same, about 15 ms**, if `plant.samples` is retuned to keep the same looks a world day | 60 looks a tick x 1000 ticks = 60,000 looks a world day today. Once a world minute, 1,440 runs a day, so about 42 looks a run gives the same 60,000. The cost is per look, not per tick. |
| `updateCamps` | 1.1 to 2.2 ms | the same if it keeps a `tick % every` beat in world time; x86.4 if left rolling every tick | `camps.js:213-242` |
| Choices and searches (`chooseTask`, `offersFor`, `bfs`, `pathToStop`, `sectorCount`) | 80 to 274 ms | **the same**. 787 to 1,139 choices and 633 to 1,218 searches a world day are driven by needs and goals, not by ticks | these counts are already per world day |
| Walk steps | 1.4 to 3.3 ms (3,479 to 8,094 steps) | **falls**. A journey of 50 tiles costs 50 ticks of walking instead of 100, and a person walks perhaps 10 minutes of a 24-hour day, so beings-on-a-path drops from 6 to 14 at once to well under 1 | `tasks.js:45-51`, 0.43 us a step |
| `runTask` `wait` countdowns and stop functions | 16 to 65 ms | **the same** if work becomes world time and progress is added on a fixed beat; x86.4 if the stop function is called every tick | `tasks.js:42-57` |
| **`updateBeing`'s per-tick head** | **28.5 to 65.7 ms** (52,029 to 83,090 passes at 0.55 to 1.01 us) | **2.5 to 5.7 s** if left per tick; **41 to 95 ms** on a once-a-world-minute beat; **1.7 to 4.0 ms** on a once-a-world-hour beat | `beings.js:334-379` |

**The conclusion.** With the cellular systems on a minute beat, the search and choice work priced per world
day, and the walk cheap, a world day costs **about 0.3 to 0.6 s** — well inside the 5 s budget — *provided
the per-tick head of `updateBeing` also moves off the tick*. If the head stays per tick, a world day is
**3 to 6.3 s**, at or over the budget with nothing left for anything else. This is the single decisive
engineering fact for the G4 plan.

### Can a being at work or asleep be skipped entirely?

A task with a declared `work` amount (`workKind`, `tasks.js:91-105`) and a task with a `wait`
(`tasks.js:44`) both end at a known number of strides, so an executor could compute the end tick and
skip the being until then. A gathering loop or a chase has no declared `work` (`design/specs/…`, section 2,
"As built (G2)") and cannot be jumped in G4.

What stops the skip today is not the task; it is the per-tick head. Everything below runs on a being whose
task has not moved:

| What runs on a being every tick | file:line | Lazy? | rng draws |

Read as a group: **the state a being carries is nearly all lazy-able** (needs, warmth, hp, thoughts,
ageing, the status string, `inDark`). What is not lazy is the set of things the *world* can do to a being
while it is not looking: fire arriving on its tile, a wolf arriving beside it, a den owner biting it, a
gnome walking past. Those are proximity events and they need a beat. A once-a-world-minute beat for the
head gives 1,440 checks a day against today's 86,400 — the sighting radii are 5 and 6 tiles and a wolf
walks 1 tile a tick, so a minute beat can miss a wolf that walks through and out again. **A once-a-world-
second check on proximity but a lazy head for state** is the shape that both budgets and rules allow; the
plan must decide the beat, and the measurement above says a bare beings scan costs 1.7 to 4.0 us a tick,
so a cheap proximity-only pass over 86,400 ticks is affordable if it stays near that floor.

## 3. Every time-valued number, grouped by the retune it needs

Conversions used below: today a tick is **86.4 world seconds**, a world day is 1000 ticks, a world hour is
41.67 ticks. A stride is 2 ticks (**2.88 world minutes**) for every species but the sprite, whose stride is
1 tick (1.44 world minutes). So `ticks(120)` is 2.88 world hours and `strides(70)` is 3.36 world hours.

Categories, as the brief names them:

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| a | a duration that converts by world time |
| b | a duration that is really a count of strides or a distance walked |
| c | a per-tick chance or amount that becomes a rate per world hour |
| d | a per-stride chance or amount |
| e | a period `every` with a phase `at`; these fix the rng stream's beat |
| f | a life stage or an age, scaled from an 84-day life in a 32-day year to a 70-year life in a 365-day year |
| g | a calendar length |
| a/b, a/f, b/e | ambiguous; see the flags after the tables |

### Is 600x feasible per frame?

600 ticks a wall second is 10 ticks a frame at 60 fps.

| Per-tick cost | 600 ticks a wall second | 10 ticks in a 16.7 ms frame |
|---|---|---|
| Today, day 20 (0.14 ms a tick) | **84 ms a second**, 8.4% of the budget | 1.4 ms a frame |
| Today, day 50 (0.38 ms a tick) | **228 ms a second**, 23% | 3.8 ms a frame |
| Today, worst day measured (0.60 ms a tick, seed r day 59) | 360 ms a second, 36% | 6.0 ms a frame |
| Under the retune's estimate (4 us a tick) | 2.4 ms a second | 0.04 ms a frame |

**Yes, 600x is feasible**, and it is feasible even at today's costs. The 200-steps-a-frame cap is never
reached: 600 ticks a second with `dt` capped at 250 ms gives at most 150 steps in one frame. Note that the
cap and the `acc = 0` reset at `main.js:11` silently discard time when it does bite, which is the mechanism
that would make a heavy world run slower than its stated speed without saying so.

The autosave is the one number that moves against the player. Today at speed 64 a world day passes every
1.3 wall seconds, so the 25 ms autosave fires every 1.3 s — 2% of the frame budget. Under G4 at 600x a world
day is 144 wall seconds, so the same 25 ms fires once every 144 s. The autosave gets 100 times cheaper.

At 1x the page runs 1 tick a second and the `acc >= 1` loop steps once a second, so a frame does almost
nothing: true realtime works as written.

## 7. What gameplay becomes without the day tier

At the default 60x a world day is 24 wall minutes; at 600x, 2.4 wall minutes. Two reference sittings:

| Sitting | At 60x | At 600x |
|---|---|---|
| 15 minutes | 0.6 world day | 6.3 world days |
| 1 hour | 2.5 world days | **25 world days** |
| 4 hours | 10 world days | 100 world days |

The season table: spring days 1-91, summer 92-182, autumn 183-273, **winter 274-365**.

### What becomes unreachable

| Thing | Under real units | At 600x |
|---|---|---|
| **A child growing up** — `LIFE.human.adult`, 16 days today, 16 **years** under the spec | 5,840 world days | **234 wall hours.** Nobody will ever see a child become an adult at the tick tier. |
| **Winter** — day 274 of a 365-day year | 273 world days | **11 wall hours.** Every rule keyed on `isWinter()` (24 sites, part 4) is dead code at the tick tier. | **Any season but spring** — summer begins on day 92 | 91 world days | 3.6 wall hours |

| **A second child from the same mother** if `birth.gap` scales with the life | 13 years | 128 wall hours |
| **A grove's sprite birth** if `grove.oldPine` (a pine counted old at 40 days today) scales with a plant's real life | a pine old at 40 years | out of reach |
| **A plant's whole cycle** — a bush dies at `plant.bushOld` = 60 days, a pine at 100 | if converted as world time, 60 and 100 world days | 2.4 and 4 wall hours |

Stated plainly for the plan: **G4 leaves the first days of a camp playable and takes the year away.**
Everything between the first fire and the first village is inside a two-hour sitting at 600x. Everything
seasonal, generational, or ecological is not. That is the price of the real clock before the day tier
exists, and it is the reason G5 is the plan that makes the game whole again.

## 8. Risks and open questions for the plan

| # | Question | Evidence | Answer or what is open |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does `tick` as a plain number stay exact? | `tick++` at `main.js:69`; `tick` is only ever incremented, added to, subtracted, compared, and taken modulo. It is never bit-masked. | **Yes.** A year is 31,536,000 ticks; 70 years is 2.21e9; `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` is 9.01e15. The engine stays exact for 285 million world years. |
| 2 | Does `nextId` stay exact? | `id: nextId++` at `beings.js:10`; reset at `main.js:7`. | **Yes.** It counts beings, not ticks; it does not scale with the clock at all. |
| 3 | Does any typed array or counter overflow at 31.5 million ticks a year? | `bfsPrev` is `Int32Array(NZ*W*H)` = 168,000 slots holding tile indices (`path.js:6`). `regionOf` is `Int32Array(W*H)` holding country ids (`field.js:9`, `snapshot.js:466`). Neither holds a tick. | **No**, with one caveat, below. |
| 4 | `bfsGen` | `let bfsGen = 0; … const gen = ++bfsGen;` (`path.js:4, 12`). It is written into a `Uint32Array` and compared against the unwrapped JS number, so past 2^32 every tile reads unseen. It is reset to 0 only in `allocSearch()`, called at `main.js:5` and `snapshot.js:451`. | **Measured 633 to 1,218 `bfs` calls a world day.** 2^32 / 1,200 is 3.58 million world days, about **9,800 world years** of tick-tier play in one unbroken session. Not a G4 risk. Worth one line in the notes so G5's century-long day-tier runs do not trip it. |
| 5 | Do thought durations count strides or ticks? | `addThought(a, key, text, value, dur)` sets `t.left = dur` (`beings.js:29-32`). `for (const t of a.thoughts) t.left--` is at `beings.js:354`, **above** the stride gate at line 379. | **Ticks, counted down once a tick.** All 57 `CLOCK.thought.*` entries are `ticks(n)` (category a). | 8 | Does any rule compare `tick % N` in a way 86,400 breaks? | 16 sites, all named: `fae.js:115`, `world.js:736`, `species.js:209, 215, 218, 222, 225, 240, 293`, `camps.js:218, 219, 224`, `main.js:71`, `gods.js:686`, plus `clock.js:29` (`hourOf`).
